ABU ROBOCON 2025

Robocon 2025: Dual Vision-Guided Basketball Robots

Role: Mechanical Designer • Team Size: 24 Members • Tools: Fusion 360, AutoCAD, MATLAB & Simulink, 3D Printers, Laser/Waterjet

Project Overview

Engineered a tactical dual-robot system designed to automate basketball passing and shooting operations under strict competition size and weight limits. The system combined a high-speed agile feeder robot with a heavy-duty precision shooter robot to achieve seamless court coverage and high-accuracy scoring.

Dual-Chassis Strategy & Structural Engineering

1. 3-Wheel Omnidirectional Chassis (High-Speed Feeder Bot)

  • Design Intent: Optimized specifically for rapid court transit, dodging opponent interference, and fast basketball retrieval.
  • Drive System: 3-wheel omni-drive setup providing 360° agility and rapid directional switching without rotating the chassis body.

2. 4-Wheel Holonomic Chassis (High-Precision Shooter Bot)

  • Design Intent: Engineered for absolute ground stability and precise positional holding while executing automated shots.
  • Chassis Architecture: Reinforced aluminum box-frame chassis designed to absorb motor recoil during flywheel acceleration.
  • Drive System: 4-wheel holonomic setup for micro-adjustments and exact alignment with the hoop target using vision feedback.
360° dual-flywheel turret shooter assembly
Lightweight carbon fiber feeder extension arm

Sub-System Mechanics & Material Selection

1. Turret Shooter Mechanism

Driven by dual Neo brushless motors, mathematically tuned in MATLAB & Simulink to deliver maximum kinetic energy transfer to the basketball without slipping. Mounted on precision ball bearings for smooth 360° pan/tilt aiming.

2. Carbon Fiber Extension System

Utilized lightweight carbon fiber tubes and laminates for the ball-feeding extension arm. Carbon fiber drastically reduced cantilever mass, preventing structural sagging and keeping the center of gravity low during high-speed maneuvers.

3. Material Framework & DFM

  • Chassis & Structural Frame: Laser-cut aluminum boxes and structural aluminum bars for a strong, custom-fabricated base.
  • High-Stress Joints & Extensions: Carbon Fiber Composites for maximum stiffness-to-weight ratio.
  • Mounting Brackets & Gears: 3D Printed PETG / PLA for rapid iteration and complex geometries.

Robocon 2026: Dual Autonomous & Manual XO-Playing Robots

Role: Mechanical Design Lead • Team Size: 24 Members • Tools: Fusion 360, AutoCAD, MATLAB & Simulink, 3D Printers, Waterjet/Laser

Project Overview

Engineered a coordinated dual-robot system—featuring one manual 4-wheel robot and one fully autonomous tracked robot. The system was designed to navigate a complex obstacle course, retrieve payload boxes, and accurately deposit them into a vertical rack matrix to execute strategic Tic-Tac-Toe (XO) game plays.

Dual-Robot Strategy & Locomotion Mechanics

1. Manual 4-Wheel Robot

A highly agile, human-controlled 4-wheel mobile platform designed for rapid court navigation and tactical gameplay assistance.

2. Autonomous Tracked Robot (Panzer-Style Drive)

  • Engineering Objective: Designed a heavy-duty continuous track drive from scratch to guarantee high ground traction and structural predictability across rugged terrain obstacles.
  • Drive & Driven Wheels: 3D-printed using rigid PETG to withstand continuous high-torque motor output without gear tooth shearing.
  • Custom Track Shoes: 3D-printed using flexible TPU (Thermoplastic Polyurethane) to deliver exceptional high friction, shock absorption, and vibration control across uneven surfaces.

Sub-System Mechanics & Materials

1. High-Precision Box Manipulator

Engineered a custom 3-axis robotic arm specifically optimized for picking modular game boxes and inserting them into tight-tolerance vertical matrix slots with minimized joint play.

2. Material Framework & DFM

  • Primary Frame: Hot-Foamed Aluminum 6061 utilized for the main chassis, providing an exceptional strength-to-weight ratio and a highly resilient foundation.
  • High-Load Brackets: Custom Carbon Fiber laminates at high-stress joint interfaces.
  • Electronics Baseplates: Laser-cut acrylic plates to isolate microcontrollers and neatly route harness wiring.

3. Vibration-Isolated Sensor Stack

Custom mounting enclosures for a Jetson AGX Xavier, ZED Depth Cameras, Cube Orange Flight Controller (on dampeners), and perimeter IR sensors for zero-latency obstacle detection.

INDUSTRIAL AUTOMATION

6-DOF Robotic Arm: Automated Industrial Spraying System

Role: Robotics Design Engineer • Tools: Fusion 360, CAD Workspace Reachability Analysis, Prototyping

Project Overview

Prototyped a 6-axis articulated robotic arm designed to fully automate adhesive and paint spraying in shoe manufacturing. The system was engineered to eliminate manual application inconsistencies, reduce material waste, and protect factory workers from hazardous chemical fumes.

6-axis articulated arm full CAD assembly
CAD workspace reachability study & singularity-free boundary map
🎥 Prototype video: Automated adhesive & paint spraying trajectory for footwear manufacturing

Engineering Breakdown

1. Manipulator Architecture

Modeled a custom 6-axis articulated arm geometry in Fusion 360, specifically engineered to replicate the complex articulation and agility of a human wrist and arm during a continuous spraying motion.

2. Workspace & Reach Analysis

Conducted comprehensive physical reachability studies within the CAD environment to ensure the end-effector could seamlessly cover the entire complex surface area of various shoe sizes and geometries without encountering mechanical singularities (kinematic dead-zones).

3. Industrial Scale-Up & Deployment

Currently transitioning the functional prototype into the main factory deployment phase, optimizing structural components and enclosures for heavy-duty factory floor durability, strict dust-proofing, and long-term chemical resistance against adhesives and paint.

ENTERPRISE PROTOTYPING

BB8 Inspired Bot: Customized Interactive Robot

Role: Mechanical Designer • Tools: Fusion 360, 3D Printing (Bambu Lab & Creality), PLA & PETG Optimization

Project Overview

Designed and prototyped a fully customized, 3D-printed BB-8 inspired robot from scratch for an enterprise office project. The primary challenge was engineering a functional, interactive droid that integrated reliable locomotion with custom mechanical features—including payload delivery and audio systems—all optimized for large-scale 3D printing.

Fully assembled 3D printed interactive BB8 droid prototype

Locomotion & Interactive Mechanics

1. Customized Waffle Pi Locomotion

Replaced traditional spherical rolling mechanics with a customized TurtleBot3 Waffle Pi base at the bottom of the robot. This provided a highly reliable, programmable mobility platform for precise navigation across office environments.

2. Automated Belly Drawer

Engineered a custom, motorized "open-and-close" drawer mechanism seamlessly integrated directly into the spherical belly, allowing the droid to securely carry and deliver items.

3. Head Speaker Integration

Modified the internal structure of the head assembly to securely house a functional speaker system, enabling interactive audio feedback while maintaining the iconic BB-8 aesthetic.

Additive Manufacturing Optimization (DFM)

Provided specialized slicing parameters (optimal layer heights, wall line counts, and infill percentages) tailored for rigid PLA and PETG filaments to maximize impact resistance while minimizing total print time.
